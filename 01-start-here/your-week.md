# Your week

The recurring meetings, what each one is for, and what's expected of you in it.

> ⚠️ **Times here are the shape of a normal week, not the authority.** Your calendar invites
> are. Meetings move — this file is so you know what each one *is* before you walk in.

---

## Meta Monday · Mon 1:00

**Stuart's whole digital team** — SEO, Email and Paid Media in one room. It frames the week.

There's no standing agenda; Alex opens a fresh doc about ten minutes beforehand. Things
flagged to **cascade** out of your team's L10 huddle travel up into this meeting through him.

**Why it matters to you more than it looks:** this is the room where you find out that
**paid and organic are competing for the same search terms.** Phil's SEO team and your team
are working the same query space from two directions. You'll build better negatives for
having sat in this meeting for a month.

**What's expected of you:** early on, nothing but attention. Later, contributing the paid
items that concern your accounts.

---

## The L10 Huddle · Tue 10:00

Your team's weekly, and the meeting where your work becomes visible. Seven timed segments,
same order every week, ending with everyone rating the meeting out of ten.

**It has its own file, and it's worth the read before your first one:**
[`the-l10-huddle.md`](../02-learning/the-l10-huddle.md).

The short version of what's expected of you: **bring a segue item from week one** (one
personal good-news thing, one work one — that's how the room learns who you are), listen,
and ask afterward about anything you didn't follow. To-dos of your own start appearing
around week four.

---

## Seton / EMEDCO weekly · Wed 2:00

The working session for that account group. You're on a **contribution track** there rather
than an ownership one — you'll audit, propose and help rather than own — so treat this one
as somewhere to learn how a big, busy account group gets run, and to bring the things your
audits turn up.

---

## Search Deep Dive · Thu 10:00–11:30

The performance review working session. Monthly, this is where the **Search Deep Dive deck**
gets built — a rolling trailing-three-month review of paid search and awareness by channel,
presented to Stuart's team.

It's also the overflow valve for the L10: anything IDS couldn't finish inside its hour can
continue here with the data in front of everyone, and experiment verdicts get their decisions.

**What's expected of you, by phase:**

| Phase | You |
|-------|-----|
| **Days 1–30** | Watch how analysts pull numbers and how the "why" gets written |
| **Days 31–60** | Co-author the PDC section — pull the numbers, draft the explanation |
| **Days 61–90** | **Own the PDC section end to end** and present it |

> The standard to write toward: a number without a mechanism behind it is noise. "Revenue
> was down 8%" is not a finding. "Revenue was down 8%, and here's the thing that caused it"
> is.

## Your 1:1 with Alex · weekly

**This one is yours to run.** Alex's 1:1s are analyst-led, which means you bring the agenda
and you drive the meeting. He doesn't prepare one for you.

That's a real adjustment if you've had managers who ran the meeting at you. Two things make
it easy:

**1. You have a ready-made agenda source.** The L10 app has a per-person 1:1 page: issues
parked to your 1:1, your to-dos, your rocks, and issues you raised. Open it before the
meeting and most of the agenda is already there.

**2. Don't save blockers for it.** If you're stuck on Tuesday, message Alex on Tuesday. The
1:1 is not the queue for getting unstuck — that's Google Chat, and using it is expected
rather than tolerated. What the 1:1 is for is the stuff that doesn't fit in a message:

| Bring | Not |
|-------|-----|
| Progress and what you learned | A status recital of things already in the to-do list |
| Decisions you need from Alex | Blockers you've been sitting on since Tuesday |
| Something structurally confusing you | Tactical questions with a quick answer |
| What you want to be better at | |
| Feedback, in both directions | |

**By phase:** at day 30 you're expected to bring an agenda and numbers. By day 60 you're
running it independently.

## The Friday professional development block · 9:00–11:00

Two hours a week, held on the calendar for learning rather than delivery. It is a real block
and not a euphemism for catch-up time.

Use it on the things that are never urgent: the parts of the fundamentals that didn't land,
a certification, a conference recap someone shared, an account you don't own. **Protect it.**
It's the first thing that gets eaten if you let it, and it's the thing that compounds.

---

## The two habits that matter more than any meeting

1. **The daily search-terms review** — about ten minutes, every business day. It is the one
   daily habit and it's where most of your early judgment gets built.
2. **Asking in chat the moment you're stuck**, rather than waiting for a scheduled slot.

Everything above is scaffolding around those two.
