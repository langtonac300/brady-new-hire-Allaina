# When you're stuck, blocked, or have nothing to do

Read this once in week one. Come back to it the first time a day goes sideways.

---

## The thing nobody tells a new person

**Some of your first two weeks will fall apart, and it will not be your fault.**

Access won't be ready. A session will get moved because something caught fire. Alex will get
pulled into a meeting and the thing you were going to do together doesn't happen. You'll
finish the reading faster than the plan assumed and it's 2pm on a Wednesday with nothing
in front of you.

Every one of those is normal, all of them are *our* planning problem rather than your
performance problem, and none of them is a reason to sit quietly and hope it resolves.

**The failure mode we actually worry about is a conscientious person losing two days rather
than saying "I'm stuck."** That is the one thing that would genuinely disappoint us. Nothing
else on this page comes close.

---

## The rules

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Stuck on something for 30 minutes** | Ask. In chat. That's the whole rule. Thirty minutes of your time is worth more than the two minutes it costs someone to answer |
| **Blocked on someone else** | Say so in chat *and* say who you're waiting on. Then pick something off the list below — don't wait idle |
| **Half a day with nothing to do** | Say that too. "I've finished X and I'm not sure what's next" is a completely normal sentence and it is information Alex needs |
| **Alex is unavailable** | **Courtney** is your peer buddy — day-to-day questions and second opinions are explicitly hers as well as his. You are not interrupting her |
| **You think the plan is wrong** | Say it. You have the only fresh pair of eyes anyone will have on this material for a year |

> **On timeboxes:** if a one-hour project is running to three, that's a scoping error on our
> side, not a failure on yours. Stop at the hour, say where you got to, and flag it. That
> feedback makes the next project better. Grinding silently makes nothing better.

## Things you can always do alone

No permission needed, no one else required, useful in any order. Pick one when you're blocked
or ahead.

### Needs account access

1. **Tour an account you don't own.** Open Seton US or EMEDCO read-only and try the T1-1
   questions on it. You'll be doing this properly later — arriving with opinions is a head
   start.
2. **A keep/kill dry run.** Pull yesterday's search terms on any account, make your own
   calls on 20 of them, and keep the list. When you do it for real you'll have a baseline
   to compare against — that's the predict-then-compare mechanic, self-served.
3. **Find something that looks wrong.** Anything. A campaign spending with no conversions, a
   name that doesn't match the convention, an ad group with one keyword. You don't need to
   be right — bring it as a question and you'll learn something either way.

### Needs nothing at all

4. **Work your questions file.** Go through
   [`04-my-work/questions/`](../04-my-work/README.md) and try to answer your own oldest
   questions from the learning files. Note which ones you *can't* answer — that list is
   genuinely valuable to Alex.
5. **Re-read [`how-brady-measures.md`](../02-learning/how-brady-measures.md).** It reads
   differently every time for the first month. That isn't wasted time; it's the single
   highest-consequence thing in the repo.
6. **Write the thing you just learned in your own words.** If you can't, you haven't got it
   yet — and finding that out now is cheap.
7. **Read a project brief that's further ahead** than where you are. You won't do it yet, but
   knowing what's coming makes the current one make more sense.
8. **Improve this repo.** Something in here will be unclear, out of date or plain wrong.
   Fixing it is real work and it's the one contribution only a new person can make — in six
   months you won't be able to see the problems any more.

## If access isn't working

This is the most common way a first week goes wrong, and it's worth being pushy about
because access takes days to fix and minutes to request.

**By the end of day one you should be able to open:** Google Ads (and see your accounts),
the MTD Spend dashboard, Google Chat, your calendar with the standing meetings on it, and
this repo.

**By the end of week two, add:** Microsoft/Bing Ads, Power BI, Adobe Analytics and Jira.
BigQuery usually takes longest because it goes through a different team — **if it hasn't
been requested by the end of week one, ask Alex about it specifically.** It's needed later
than the others but it has the longest lead time, so it's the one that quietly bites.

**If something on that list doesn't work: say so the same day, not at your next 1:1.** Nobody
will think you're complaining. Waiting a week to mention it is the only version of this that
causes a problem.

## One thing to hold on to

Being confused for the first month is the job working correctly, not you doing it badly. The
plan expects you to be confused — that's why nothing you touch in month one can break
anything.

The only way to get this wrong is to be confused *quietly*.
