# About how you work

Twenty minutes if you move, in your first couple of days — longer is fine, and so is leaving
things blank. Bring it to your first conversation with Alex.

> **This is not a test and it isn't stored anywhere formal.** The whole ramp was designed
> before anyone had met you, which means it's built on guesses. This is how we replace the
> guesses with facts and change the plan where it's wrong.
>
> **Honest answers make the next twelve weeks better. Flattering ones make them worse** — if
> you say you're comfortable with something you're not, we'll skip past it and you'll be
> quietly stuck in week six.
>
> It pairs with [`../05-self-assessment/baseline.md`](../05-self-assessment/baseline.md),
> which covers *what you can do*. This one is about *how you like to work*.

**The levers are real.** One answer in section 1 gets your BigQuery access requested in week
one, long before anyone thinks you need it — that one has a lead time measured in weeks and it
is the access that most often bites. One answer in section 2 can replace a week of reading
with a week of shadowing. There's a
table at the end — [what your answers actually change](#what-your-answers-actually-change) —
listing every lever this sheet controls. Read it first if you want to know why any of these
questions are being asked.

---

## How to fill this in

- **Tick tables** by putting an `x` in the cell. Ranking tables take a number.
- **Written answers** go under the `>` line. A sentence is plenty. Nobody is reading this for
  style.
- **Blank is a real answer.** It means "no strong feeling," and that's useful too.
- **If you only have ten minutes,** do sections 1, 2 and 3. Those are the three that change
  the most.

## 3 · When something isn't going well

**When you get stuck on something, what do you usually do first?**

There's no right answer, but there is a consequence: **the last one is the pattern we most
want to catch early**, because it's how a good week quietly becomes a lost one. If that's
you, say so — Alex will check in more often rather than waiting for you to raise a hand.

**Where would you rather ask?**

| | ✓ |
|---|:---:|
| The team chat channel, where anyone can answer | |

This one is worth being honest about. [The 30-minute
rule](./when-youre-stuck-or-idle.md) — stuck for half an hour, ask in chat — is the single
most load-bearing habit in your first month, and it quietly fails if asking in a channel full
of people you've just met feels expensive. **If that's the case, we start you in DMs and move
to the channel when you're ready.** That's a normal path, not a concession.

*(Third row is a fair preference, but it's the one we'd push back on: see
[`your-week.md`](./your-week.md) — the 1:1 is deliberately not the queue for getting
unstuck.)*

**How does it feel to be wrong in front of someone?** Not a character question — a scheduling
one. A lot of this ramp is **predict-then-compare**: you write down what you think the answer
is, then look. T1-1 asks for a prediction before you open the account, and

That mechanic works brilliantly and it does mean being visibly wrong on purpose, fairly often,
in front of your manager in week one.

>

**If you had too much on, how would we know?** Some people go quiet. Some people ask more
questions. Some people say it plainly. Tell us your tell, because the thing we most want to
avoid is a good week disappearing silently.

## What your answers actually change

Every row here is a real lever, and pulling it costs us nothing in week one and quite a lot in
week six. That's the entire reason for the sheet.

| If you say | What we change |
|---|---|
| Reading isn't how you learn | More pairing and shadowing, files demoted to lookup. This is the biggest single change available |
| You've never used SQL | BigQuery access requested in week one — it's the longest lead time of anything you'll be given |
| Big spreadsheets aren't second nature | A session on it before Tier 1, rather than mid-project |
| You grind rather than ask | Alex checks in proactively instead of waiting for a raised hand |
| Asking in a public channel feels expensive | You start in DMs and move to the channel when you want to. Your daily three lines can go to Alex directly instead of the team channel |
| You want feedback written down | Project debriefs come back as notes, not just as conversation |
| Being wrong in front of people is uncomfortable | Predict-then-compare stays, because it's how the learning works — but the early rounds are solo, and Alex goes first on the paired ones |
| Deadlines help you | Briefs come with dates. If they don't help, they come with an order instead |
| You'd rather listen in meetings first | You get a heads-up before you're asked anything, and the Deep Dive handover is rehearsed rather than cold |
| Mornings are your good hours | Heavy reading moves to mornings and paired work to afternoons — or the reverse |
| You've worked in SEO | Meta Monday becomes a contribution meeting for you much sooner |
| You've barely used AI tools | The AI thread starts at the beginning and moves at its own pace |
| There's something you want that isn't in the plan | We look for where it fits. Some of it will fit inside the 90 days, some belongs in [`after-the-ramp.md`](../03-projects/after-the-ramp.md) |

**Anything you disagree with in this table is worth saying too.** If you can see a change we'd
make that you'd hate, that's exactly the kind of thing this conversation is for.

## What happens with this

You and Alex talk it through in your first proper conversation. **Some of your answers will
change the plan** — the reading order, the pace, how paired sessions run, when we get you into
BigQuery.

**Then, once, at about day 30: re-read your own answers.** Fifteen minutes, on your own. Some
of them will look wrong to you by then — you'll have learned things about how this job works
that you couldn't have known in week one. Tell Alex which ones changed, and we adjust again.
That's the whole follow-up. There isn't a third pass.

Other than that it gets left alone. It isn't scored, it doesn't go into any review, and nobody
else needs to see it.
