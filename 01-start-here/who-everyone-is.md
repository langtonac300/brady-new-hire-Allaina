## Your team

### Alex Langton — Paid Media Manager · your manager

Owns paid media across the Americas — Brady, Seton, EMEDCO, PDC, Mecco, Amazon. Your 1:1 is
**analyst-led**, meaning you bring the agenda, and he doesn't push work through a ticket
tracker. **Practical consequence: Google Chat is the expected way to get unblocked, not a
last resort.** If you're stuck at 10am, message him at 10am.

He builds systems rather than one-off documents — this repo is an example — and he'll ask
you to leave something behind at the end of most projects for the same reason.

### Courtney Hamilton — Senior Paid Search Specialist · your peer buddy

Moving toward a **strategist** role: Brady Mexico, Canada and Brazil, plus social
(LinkedIn, Facebook, Reddit) and trade publications. She led the Apple Ads launch. She's the
person to ask "is this normal?" without it costing you anything.

**On her register:** ALL-CAPS enthusiasm, "im dead / im obsessed," countdowns to margarita
hour, and she will start an office scavenger hunt unprompted. There's a long-running bit
about her eyesight — she once couldn't identify something at 12× zoom and the team has never
let it go. Dogs **Goose** and **Blu** (she wanted "Bleu"; her husband vetoed the spelling).
She would like a dog-friendly office more than she'd like most things.

> ⚠️ **Sharp sarcasm is her default setting, not a signal.** New people occasionally read
> friction into it. There isn't any.

### Scott Palmersheim — Paid Search Specialist

Currently on **PDC Healthcare and Wristbands** — the accounts moving to you — and heading
toward **Seton US, EMEDCO and Seton Canada**. He also owns the trade-publication budget and
has built a set of his own automation tools on Google's AI stack.

**On his register:** the quiet one, until a very well-timed dry one-liner lands. The room's
running joke is that Scott is the team's child, complete with periodic child-labor-law

### CJ Weissenberger — Paid Search Specialist

Currently **Seton US, EMEDCO and Seton Canada**, plus **Amazon** day to day, and moving over

## Up the chain

### Stuart MacKay — Sr. Digital Marketing Manager · your skip-level

Alex's manager. Your work has to eventually show up in numbers Stuart cares about: revenue,
non-brand quality traffic, efficiency inside guardrails, and **the reason a number moved**.

The one line of his worth internalizing early:

> *"You either get the opportunity to make a recommendation or you'll get terms dictated to
> you."*

That's the whole reason your Day-90 target is "brings recommendations, not open questions."
He runs **GSD** meetings — Get Shit Done: pick a topic, debate it properly, agree the next
step, then everyone commits including whoever lost the argument.

**On his register:** **"Stu(pid) fact of the week"**, a Friday ritual in the team chat, past
entries including how pigeons navigate by liver and the size of Australia's largest recorded
cockroach seizure. His signature move is the one-word reply — a fully-formed question met
with *"no"*. Roasting runs both directions between him and the whole paid team, and he
defends their actual work fiercely inside the same breath as the joke.

Two levels above you, in Camden. Stuart's manager. Four functions report to him: Analytics,

## One note on the jokes

The team chat is a running roast and it will look impenetrable for a few weeks. Two things
worth knowing:

1. **You don't have to be funny to belong here.** Nobody is scoring your contributions.
2. **The roasting and the work are separate.** People who spend the morning trading insults
   will defend each other's work without hesitation in the next meeting. If the register
   ever reads as hostile, it isn't — and Alex or Courtney will tell you the same thing if
   you ask.

If something in this file turns out to be wrong or out of date, fix it. You'll meet these
people with fresh eyes, which is the one time anybody notices.
