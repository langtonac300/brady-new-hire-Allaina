## The point

Run T2-1 again, on a different account group, and notice what changes.

Two things make this a genuinely different exercise rather than a repeat:

**The catalog is broader.** PDC Healthcare sells into a fairly narrow world. Seton and
EMEDCO sell safety and identification products across a much wider range of industries,
which means far more search terms sit in the "could plausibly be a customer" grey zone. Your
Keep/Kill rules from T2-1 will not all survive contact.

**The loop is young.** The Seton/EMEDCO search-terms process is new — months old, not years.
The Brady US equivalent has been running long enough to be mature. You are looking at a
process still bedding in, which means some of what you find isn't a bad call by anyone; it's
coverage that hasn't been built yet.

That second point is the real reason this project exists. **Telling "someone decided this"
apart from "nobody has got to this yet" is a judgment skill**, and it's far easier to learn
on a young process than to reverse-engineer on a mature one.

## What to do

1. Confirm with Alex **which account** to pull from — Seton US and EMEDCO behave differently
   enough that it matters, and it may depend on what's live that week.
2. Pull **50 search terms**, same method as T2-1: recent window, mixed across the spend
   range, cost and conversion columns hidden before you start.
3. Call every row Keep/Kill with a one-line reason. Same table, same rules.
4. **Then, and only then**, look at how the existing negatives are set up for that account
   group — what lists exist, what they cover, and what they don't.
5. Write **one observation about the loop itself.** Not about a single term — about the
   process. Something like: which category of term keeps getting through, and what a list
   would have to look like to catch it.
6. If you can, skim the mature Brady US setup for contrast. It's read-only for you and it is
   the reference implementation — the point is to see what "finished" looks like.

---

## ⚠️ You're a contributor here, not the owner

Seton and EMEDCO are not your accounts. Someone else owns them, and they're going through
their own transition.

That changes how you write this up. Findings get **routed to Alex**, framed as "here's what
I saw, here's what I'd suggest" — not as instructions, and not posted directly to whoever
runs the account. Getting this etiquette right early matters more than the findings
themselves; it's the difference between a helpful second pair of hands and a new person
telling an experienced one how to do their job.

## ⚠️ Don't go structural

You will notice things about how the campaigns themselves are organized. Some of it will
look untidy.

Leave it. The account structure here may change substantially, and deep archaeology on it is
time you won't get back. Search terms, negatives and the conversion basket are the parts
worth learning — they matter regardless of what happens to the structure around them.

---

## What good looks like

- You can point at a **rule from T2-1 that didn't transfer**, and explain what about this
  account group broke it.
- Your loop observation is about a **pattern**, not a row. "Terms about installation and
  repair services keep coming through, and there's no list covering them" is an observation.
  "This one term should be killed" is just a row.
- You separated "this is a gap" from "this is a choice" — or, where you couldn't tell,
  you asked instead of assuming.
- Nothing in your write-up reads as a criticism of a person.
