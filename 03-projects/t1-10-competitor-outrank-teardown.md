## The point

Wristbands explicitly bids to **outrank two named competitors** — you won't find this
tactic anywhere else in the estate. It's a bidding strategy that takes a *competitive*
target rather than an efficiency one: instead of aiming at a return or a cost per
acquisition, it aims at appearing above a specific other advertiser.

That's an unusual thing to point an account at, and it's a good first exercise in the
question you'll be asked over and over: **is this earning its place?**

This is also your first project that ends in a recommendation rather than a description.
That's a step up, and it's meant to be.

---

## What to do

1. **Find it.** Locate the campaigns using the outrank-share strategy and note which
   competitors are named.
2. **Work out what it's doing to cost per click.** Compare against something — the same
   campaigns before the strategy was applied, or a comparable campaign in the account that
   isn't using it. State what you compared and over what window.
3. **Work out what it's doing to volume.** Impression share, clicks, conversions.
4. **Write the recommendation.** Keep it, change it, or drop it — with the number that
   supports your call.

---

## ⚠️ Two traps

**"It's expensive" is not a recommendation.** Every bidding decision is a trade. The
recommendation has to say what you'd give up and what you'd get: *"Keep it, but the CPC
premium is X% against the rest of the account, so I'd want to see it earning that back on
volume before it expands."*

**"The previous owner set it up" is not evidence either way.** It may be a deliberate
strategic choice with a business reason you can't see from the account, and it may be
something that made sense two years ago. **Form your own view from the numbers first, then
ask.** Doing it in that order is how you find out whether there's a reason — asking first
just gets you someone else's answer.

---

## ⚠️ Read-only

Do not change the bidding strategy, the targets, or the named competitors. This is a week-3
reading exercise on a live account that spends real money.

---

## What good looks like

- A recommendation with a number attached, and a named comparison behind that number.
- You can explain what an outrank-share strategy is actually competing on — and why that's
  different from an efficiency target.
- You have a view on what you'd want to *measure* to settle the question properly, even if
  you couldn't measure it in an hour. Naming the experiment you'd want is a real answer.
