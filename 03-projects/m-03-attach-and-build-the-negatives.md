## The point

There's a shared negative list on this account that **isn't attached to anything**. A
negative list that isn't attached does nothing at all — it's the paid-search equivalent of
a smoke alarm still in its box.

Fixing that is a two-minute change. The rest of this project is the more interesting half:
building Mecco's *own* negatives from its *own* search terms, which is the first time you'll
do the daily Keep/Kill loop as a build rather than a review.

---

## What to do

### 1 · Read the list before you attach it

Open it. Read the actual terms in it. Two questions: does it still make sense, and is it
doing what its name says?

### 2 · Attach it

To the campaigns that should have it. Note which ones and why.

### 3 · Build Mecco's own

- Pull Mecco's search terms for as far back as the account has spent.
- Run each one through the Keep/Kill question: **could a person typing this ever be shopping
  for what we sell?** Not "did it convert" — that's a different job, and on an account this
  small there isn't enough conversion data to answer it anyway.
- Build a first Mecco-specific negative list from the kills.

### 4 · Propose, then apply

Post the proposed list to the change thread with your reasoning. Apply it after review.

## ⚠️ Read the list before attaching it — properly

On Seton and EMEDCO, roughly **half** the shared lists labeled "negative keywords" aren't
hygiene lists at all. They exist to *route* traffic between campaigns — deliberately pushing
a search out of one campaign so a different one picks it up.

Attach a routing list to the wrong campaign and you block traffic you wanted. The tell is
the contents: a list full of things the business actually sells is doing routing, not
blocking.

Mecco's is probably straightforward. **Check anyway** — it takes two minutes, and the habit
is what you're building.

---

## ⚠️ Negatives are easy to add and easy to regret

The easiest change in paid search to make, and one of the easier ones to get quietly wrong.

- **Match type matters on negatives too**, and it works differently than you'd expect. A
  broad negative blocks more than you think.
- **A negative can block a term you want** through a shared word. This is the classic way a
  new analyst quietly kills a good keyword.
- **When you're unsure, go narrower.** A phrase negative you have to add twice is much
  cheaper than a broad one that blocks a product line for a month before anyone notices.

Check your proposed negatives against the account's *existing* keywords before you post
them. If a negative would block one of your own keywords, you'll see it there.

---

## What good looks like

- The list is attached, to campaigns you can justify.
- The first build is applied, and each kill has a one-line reason.
- You can say roughly **how much spend this would have blocked over the last 30 days** —
  even approximately. That number is what makes the work legible to someone else.
- You checked your negatives against existing keywords, and you say so.
