## The point

Every number at Brady has three questions attached to it whether or not anyone asks them
out loud: **which source, which attribution model, and is there a known problem with it?**

This hour is where that stops being advice and becomes a habit. You'll do it on a real
slide that real people saw, which is considerably more sobering than doing it on a made-up
one.

---

## What to do

1. **Alex gives you one real Search Deep Dive slide.** Any month.
2. **Go through every number on it** — including the ones in chart axes and footnotes, not
   just the big ones in boxes. For each:

   | Column | What goes in it |
   |--------|-----------------|
   | The number | As it appears on the slide |
   | Source | Which system or report it came out of |
   | Model | First-touch? Platform conversions? Something else? |
   | Caveat | What would make this number misleading, if anything |

3. **Mark anything you can't source.** Explicitly. A row that says *"I don't know where
   this came from"* is a completed row.

---

## ⚠️ "I can't source this one" is the right answer

Do not guess, and do not reason your way to a plausible source. A confidently-wrong
attribution is worse than a blank, because a blank gets checked and a plausible answer
doesn't.

This matters more here than almost anywhere else on your ramp: the fastest way to lose
credibility in this job is to state a number's provenance confidently and be wrong. The
fastest way to build it is to say "I'd want to check that one" in a room full of people who
didn't.

---

## What good looks like

- Every number has all four columns attempted, and **at least one honest unknown**. If you
  sourced all of them on your first try, you were guessing on some.
- You can state the first-touch vs. platform-conversions distinction out loud, without
  opening the cheat sheet.
- You spotted at least one number where the model matters — where quoting it under the
  other model would tell a materially different story.

**Keep this one.** Come back to it in the last month, when you're the person writing the
Deep Dive section rather than annotating it, and see how much of your own work you could
source.
