---

## The point

"Is this inside the guardrail?" is a question you'll be asked in meetings, and the answer
is less mechanical than it looks. Different account groups run to different ceilings,
non-brand is measured on a different attribution view entirely, and the source data isn't
always right.

Ten reps in an hour is enough to make the shape stick.

---

## What to do

1. **Get the current guardrails from Alex before you start.** They move as plans get
   finalised, and they differ by account group:

   | Account group | Shape of the target |
   |---|---|
   | Brady paid search | The tightest — the most efficient major group |
   | Seton / EMEDCO | Much looser, structurally, by design |
   | Brady US non-brand | Looser still — **and only meaningful on the IP view**, not first-touch |

   Don't take numbers for these out of this repo. Ask, and write down what you were told and
   when.
2. **Alex hands you ten real account-months** pulled from the team's budget and spend
   sheets.
3. **For each one, make the call:** in guardrail or not. Write down the number you used,
   the guardrail you compared it against, and which attribution view that comparison needs.
4. **Then flag anything in the inputs that looked off to you.** Anything: a number that
   doesn't fit its neighbours, a figure that seems attached to the wrong account, a month
   that doesn't behave like the others.

---

## ⚠️ Step 4 is not padding

Brady's budget and spend sheets have, at least once, had **two accounts' figures
accidentally swapped**. It happens, and it's documented in
[`how-brady-measures.md`](../02-learning/how-brady-measures.md).

**A calculation done perfectly on a bad input is still wrong** — and it's wrong in the most
expensive way, because it looks right all the way to the meeting. Checking whether the
inputs are plausible before you do arithmetic on them is a real skill, and this is the
cheapest place you will ever practice it.

If nothing looks off to you, say that. Don't invent a problem to have an answer.

---

## ⚠️ A guardrail is a ceiling, not a target

This one runs against most people's instincts. If an account is **inside** its guardrail and
**behind** on pacing, the move at Brady is usually to **push spend, not cut it**. Room under
the ceiling is unspent opportunity, not a buffer to protect.

Don't build a reflexive "cut to protect efficiency" habit. It's the opposite of how the team
wants accounts managed, and it's hard to unlearn once it sets.

---

## What good looks like

- Ten calls, each showing the number you used and the guardrail you compared it to.
- For each one, you say **which view** it needs — and you flagged the non-brand rows as
  needing IP rather than first-touch.
- You raised at least one "that looks odd to me" — or you can explain why you're confident
  nothing was.
