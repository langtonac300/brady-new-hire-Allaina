## This one doesn't have an end date

It's not a project with a deliverable and a readout. It's the first thing you own — and it
arrives at around day 45, deliberately, because **day 60 is a long time to go without having
been trusted with anything.**

Mecco is small enough that ownership is real rather than symbolic. The stakes are low, the
responsibility isn't.

---

## What owning it means, week to week

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pacing** | Is it on track for the month? Per business day, the way the rest of Brady reports |
| **Search terms** | The Keep/Kill loop on this account is yours. Not "helping with" — yours |
| **Optimization** | Keyword-level and landing-page-level work on the transition landing page |
| **Reporting** | You bring it to your 1:1 **without being asked** |

---

## ⚠️ That last row is the actual test

Anyone can do the work. Ownership is that **the update arrives before the question does.**

The difference between "here's what happened on Mecco" and "how's Mecco doing?" is the
entire distinction between an analyst who does tasks and one who owns an account. It's also
what day 61–90 is measured on.

**If a week was quiet, say so.** *"Nothing moved, spend was $X against a target of $Y, and
here's why that's fine"* is a complete update. Ownership doesn't mean manufacturing news —
it means nobody has to ask.

---

## ⚠️ Ownership isn't a license to skip review

While you're in the Assist phase, **live changes on Mecco still get proposed in the thread
before they ship**, same as anywhere else. What's changed is that the agenda is yours: you
decide what's worth doing, you bring the proposal, you carry it through.

The review disappears as a track record builds on each kind of change — not on a date.

---

## ⚠️ The ground will move under you

Mecco is mid-rebrand from MECCO to Brady, with a transition landing page. Things about this
account **will change while you're holding it** — URLs, messaging, possibly structure.

That's a feature. Managing an account through a live transition is a genuinely different
skill from managing a stable one, and most people don't get to practice it on something
small.

**Flag changes you notice rather than just absorbing them.** Noticing that something moved,
and saying so, is part of the job.
