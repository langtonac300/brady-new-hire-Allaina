## The point

PDC Healthcare is a tidy account. Most accounts are not, and Seton US is the clearest
example on the team of what "not tidy" looks like at scale.

The useful thing here isn't cataloguing the mess — it's working out **how an account gets
that way**, because it happens to every long-running account eventually, including the ones
you'll own. This is a lesson you'll use for the rest of your career, and it's why this is
project number two rather than project number twenty.

---

## What to do

1. **Open Seton US.** Different manager account: **"Brady US - MCC (Seton/Emed)"**. Yes,
   that MCC does not contain Brady US. Yes, really.
2. **Run the T1-1 tour again** — campaigns that spent in the last 30 days, with type,
   bidding strategy, what they're optimizing to, brand or non-brand.
3. **Then do one extra thing.** Count two numbers:
   - how many campaigns got impressions in the last 30 days
   - how many campaigns exist in the account and aren't *removed*

   Write both down. The gap is the interesting part.
4. **Answer the question:** why does this account look nothing like PDC? Get to three
   structural differences, each with a reason attached.

---

## ⚠️ Don't switch anything on

There is a lot of paused structure in here. **Never bulk-enable anything paused** — some of
it is inert history and some of it is off for a reason that isn't visible in the campaign
name. Reading is free; enabling is not.

---

## What good looks like

- Three differences, each with a **reason**. "It's bigger" is an observation, not a reason.
- One of your reasons should account for most of that gap in step 3. You'll get there
  yourself if you sort the non-spending campaigns by when they were created and look at how
  far back they go.
- You can say what a newer account would have to do differently to avoid ending up the same
  way — and whether that's even possible.

Bring your answer to the readout **before** asking Alex what the answer is. Being wrong here
and finding out why is worth more than being told first.
