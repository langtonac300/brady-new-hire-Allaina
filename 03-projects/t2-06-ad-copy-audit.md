## The point

An **RSA** — responsive search ad — isn't one ad. You supply up to 15 headlines and 4
descriptions, and Google assembles combinations of them per auction. You're not writing an
ad; you're writing **parts that have to work in any order**.

That has two consequences most people miss:

- Any headline can appear next to any other. Two that say nearly the same thing will
  sometimes appear together and read like a stutter.
- Anything that depends on position — a headline that only makes sense first — will
  eventually appear somewhere else.

This audit covers three things: **asset strength** (has Google got enough distinct material
to work with), **pinning** (are we overriding the assembly, and should we be), and
**brand compliance** (does the copy follow Brady's standards).

---

## What to do

1. Pull the RSAs from PDC Healthcare. Work ad group by ad group.
2. **Asset strength.** For each ad, note how many headlines and descriptions exist, and
   Google's own strength rating. Then judge for yourself whether the assets are genuinely
   *distinct* — a rating counts assets, it doesn't notice that six of them are the same
   claim reworded.
3. **Pinning.** Pinning locks an asset to a fixed position. Note every pin and ask what it's
   for:

   | Pinning is defensible when | Pinning is a problem when |
   |---|---|
   | A legal or compliance line must always show | It's there because someone liked that headline |
   | A required disclaimer needs a fixed slot | So much is pinned that Google can't test anything |

   Heavy pinning turns a responsive ad back into a static one. Sometimes that's the intent.
   Often it's accumulated habit — worth asking which.
4. **Brand compliance.** Check against Brady's standards: **AP style, no Oxford comma, no
   emoji, no slang.** These are binding for customer-facing output, not stylistic
   preferences.
5. **Rewrite five headlines.** Pick five that are genuinely weak — not the five easiest —
   and rewrite them. For each, one line on what was wrong and what your version does better.
6. Note anything you can't check yourself, especially product claims. Route those to Alex
   rather than guessing.

---

## ⚠️ Character limits are part of the craft

Headlines cap at 30 characters and descriptions at 90. A rewrite that's better but two
characters over isn't a rewrite — it won't run.

Count them. Then read each one aloud: if it only parses because you know what it meant,
it's too compressed.

## ⚠️ Don't invent a product claim

The fastest way to cause a real problem here is to write a headline that sounds great and
asserts something untrue — a certification we don't hold, a shipping speed we don't offer, a
material we don't use.

**If you can't verify a claim from the site or from someone who knows, don't write it.**
"Sounds like the kind of thing we'd say" is not verification. This is the one part of the
project where being wrong has consequences outside the account.

---

## What good looks like

- You judged asset *distinctness* yourself instead of reporting Google's strength rating as
  the finding.
- For every pin, you can say whether it's deliberate or inherited — or that you asked.
- Your five rewrites are within character limits, and you counted rather than estimated.
- Every claim in your rewrites is one you can source.
- You noticed at least one pair of headlines that would read badly **together**. That's the
  RSA-specific failure mode, and spotting it means you've understood the format rather than
  just proofreading it.
