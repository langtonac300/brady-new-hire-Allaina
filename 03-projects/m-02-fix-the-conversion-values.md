## This one is your first real live change

Not a sandbox, not a mock-up — a change to a live account that stays there.

It's deliberately this one. It's a change a beginner can reason about correctly with a few
weeks of training, on an account small enough that being wrong is cheap, and it makes every
downstream number on that account mean something for the first time. There isn't a better
first change available anywhere in the estate.

---

## What to do

### 1 · Inventory what's there

List Mecco's conversion actions and their current values. They're **$1 placeholders** — a
number someone typed to get past a required field, not an estimate of anything.

While every value is $1, the account's ROAS is really just a conversion count wearing a
dollar sign.

### 2 · Find out what a Mecco lead is actually worth

Not what you'd like it to be worth — what happens to it. Where does a Mecco lead go, who
picks it up, what's the realistic outcome and how often? **Ask Alex.** This is his account;
the answer is in his head, not in the platform.

### 3 · Don't invent a number — find the house convention

There's an existing convention across the estate for valuing lead actions. **Go and look at
how PDC and Seton assign values to theirs.** You'll find that different lead types carry
different values — a phone call, a form fill and a quote request are not treated as
equivalent, and the relationships between them are deliberate.

Follow the *shape* of that convention, adjusted for what you learned in step 2. Borrowing a
reasoned house standard beats inventing a number, and it means your values are comparable
to everything else the team reports.

### 4 · Write the value model

Each conversion action: current value, proposed value, and **one line of reasoning a
non-marketer could follow**.

### 5 · Propose it, then apply it

Post it to the proposed-change thread. Once Alex has reviewed it, **you make the change.**

---

## ⚠️ Write down the date you applied it

**Conversion values are not retroactive.** Everything recorded before your change keeps the
old value; everything after gets the new one. So any before/after comparison that crosses
that date is comparing two different measuring sticks, and it will look like a dramatic
improvement that is entirely an artifact of your edit.

Note the date in the account's change history and on anything you report from this account
for the next few months. You will be very glad of this in about six weeks.

---

## ⚠️ Check the bidding strategy before you predict the effect

Look at what Mecco's campaigns are actually bidding on before you say what this change will
do.

If the account is on **manual bidding**, fixing the values changes what you can *see* — it
doesn't change a single bid by itself. That's not a small outcome: it's the **prerequisite**
for the account ever moving to value-based bidding, and nothing else can happen until it's
true.

If the account is on a **value-based smart bidding strategy**, the change does feed the
algorithm, and it'll need time to settle. Say which case you're in.

---

## ⚠️ A reasoned wrong value beats $1

Don't stall trying to get this perfect. A value you can defend in one sentence, that's in
the right order of magnitude, is enormously better than a placeholder — and it can be
revised once there's real data behind it. Perfect is not on the table here; defensible is.

---

## What good looks like

- Every proposed value has a one-line reason a non-marketer could follow.
- You can say what you expect to change on the account afterwards — **and what won't.**
- The date is written down.
- You applied it yourself. This one's yours.
