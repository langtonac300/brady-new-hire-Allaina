## The point

Mecco is small, it's Alex's own account, and it's **half-built**. That combination is hard
to find and worth a lot: there is real, visible, unambiguous improvement available to
someone in their first six weeks, and the spend is small enough that being wrong costs
almost nothing.

The skill being trained is **going into an account cold and finding what's wrong** — and
then, harder, **ranking what you found by what it actually costs** rather than by how much
it annoyed you.

⚠️ **MECCO ≠ EMEDCO.** Different account, different account group, easy to typo, and the
mistake is embarrassing rather than dangerous. Check the account name at the top of the
screen before you write anything down.

---

## What to do

Go through the account and write down everything that's wrong. **Rank the list by cost.**

Two things are already known, so start there and then keep going:

| Known defect | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Conversion actions carry **$1 placeholder values** | Every ROAS and value number on the account is meaningless. Fixed in [M-2](./m-02-fix-the-conversion-values.md) |
| A shared negative list **exists but isn't attached** to anything | It's doing nothing at all. Fixed in [M-3](./m-03-attach-and-build-the-negatives.md) |

**Then look for what isn't on that list.** Suggestions for where to point:

- campaign structure — does it make sense, or is it one campaign doing four jobs?
- budgets against actual spend — is anything capped, is anything not spending at all?
- match types and keyword coverage
- search terms — is it buying things it shouldn't be?
- ad copy against Brady's brand standards (AP style, no Oxford comma, no emoji)
- landing pages — the account is mid-rebrand from MECCO to Brady, so check where ads actually
  land and whether it's where they should
- anything disapproved, limited, or silently not serving
- conversion tracking beyond the values — is it firing at all, and once?

---

## ⚠️ Rank by cost, not by how visible it is

This is the part people get wrong, and it's the reason the deliverable is a *ranked* list
rather than a list.

A $1 placeholder conversion value makes every efficiency number on the account meaningless —
which means nobody can make a correct decision about the account at all. A typo in a headline
is far more visible and costs almost nothing. **The placeholder outranks the typo.**

For each row, say what it costs: money, or the ability to trust a number. Both are costs.

---

## ⚠️ Write, don't fix

Not because you'd break it — because the fixes are the next three projects, and doing them
before you've seen the whole picture means you'll fix them in the wrong order.

---

## You're done when

- [ ] Every defect row says three things: **what's wrong, what it costs, how you'd fix it**
- [ ] The list is **ranked by cost** (money or trust in a number), and the ranking is defensible to someone who disagrees
- [ ] You found **at least one thing not on the known list** — likely, on a half-built account
- [ ] Anything you suspected but couldn't confirm is marked **unconfirmed**
- [ ] You **wrote, didn't fix** — the fixes are M-2 and M-3, in order
- [ ] Standard wrap — prediction first, written up the same day
