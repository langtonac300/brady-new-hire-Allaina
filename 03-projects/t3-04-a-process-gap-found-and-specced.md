## The point

T3-2 asked where money leaks. This asks a different question about the same machinery: **where
is the process that's supposed to catch that money thin, silent, or simply not running?**

The negatives loop is the recurring process you know best — you've fed it (T2-2) and you've
seen an account where a negative list existed but was **attached to nothing** and so did
nothing at all (M-3, on Mecco). That last one is the shape you're hunting at scale: a control
that looks present and isn't actually working.

A process gap is worth more than a single waste finding because it **keeps producing waste
until someone closes it.** Finding one, mapping it, and writing down what it'd take to fix is
exactly the kind of second-year, high-leverage work `after-the-ramp.md` points at — you're
just doing the *diagnose-and-spec* half of it now, not the build.

---

## What to do

### 1 · Map where the loop actually runs

For each account group you can see, establish the truth, not the assumption:

| Question | How you'd tell |
|---|---|
| **Does the loop run here at all?** | Is there a live search-terms review process, or does nothing sweep this account group? |
| **If it runs, does it *reach* the account?** | The M-3 trap — a list can exist and be attached to nothing. Check the negatives are actually attached to serving campaigns, not just sitting in a shared library |
| **Does it cover the whole group?** | New campaigns, new ad groups — do they inherit the protection, or do they start naked until someone remembers? |

Write it as a simple coverage grid: account group × runs / reaches / covers-new. The blanks
are your candidates.

### 2 · Pick the one that costs the most, and confirm it's real

Don't spec the first blank you find — spec the one leaking the most. A group with no loop and
real spend beats a cosmetic gap on a dormant account. Confirm the gap is genuine before you
build a case on it: "I think this isn't covered" becomes "here are three recent expensive
terms that would have been caught and weren't."

### 3 · Spec the fix — for a human reader, not a machine

One page: **what the gap is, what it's costing (with evidence), and what it would take to
close it.** Not code. The spec is "here's the hole, here's the size of it, here's roughly the
work" — enough for Alex to decide whether it's worth doing and who does it.

---

## ⚠️ Absence is hard to prove — say how sure you are

The whole project is about finding things that *aren't* there, and "I couldn't find it" and
"it doesn't exist" are different claims. You may be looking in the wrong place, or a process
may run somewhere you can't see. So grade your confidence: *"no coverage that I can find, and
I checked A, B and C"* is honest and useful. *"No coverage"* stated flat may just be a gap in
your access. When you're not sure, the finding is a **question for Alex**, not a conclusion.

## ⚠️ You're specifying, not building — and definitely not attaching anything live

Attaching a negative list, changing a shared library, wiring up a review — those are live
changes with the silent-over-blocking risk you already know. This project stops at the spec.
The building is a real piece of work with its own review; don't fold it into this one because
you can see how to do it.

---

## You're done when

- [ ] A coverage grid distinguishing **doesn't run / runs but doesn't reach / runs but doesn't cover new campaigns** — three gaps, three fixes
- [ ] The gap you specced is the one with the **most spend behind it**, proven with specific recent terms — not a hunch
- [ ] A one-page spec: the hole, its size (with evidence), and roughly the work — a manager could decide go/no-go without three follow-ups
- [ ] Confidence graded on any absence ("no coverage I can find, checked A/B/C") — unsure findings raised as **questions**, not conclusions
- [ ] You **specced, didn't build** — nothing attached or wired live
- [ ] Standard wrap
