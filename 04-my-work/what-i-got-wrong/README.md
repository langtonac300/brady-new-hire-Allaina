# What I got wrong

One running file. One line per surprise. All ninety days.

**Nobody reads this but you.** It isn't graded, it doesn't go into any review, and the
blunter it is the more it's worth.

---

## Why bother

Two reasons, and the second is the real one.

**1. Patterns show up that no single project reveals.** Any one wrong prediction is just a
thing you didn't know yet. Four of them together tend to show a *habit* — you keep trusting
the platform number, or you keep assuming two accounts behave alike because they sell similar
things. A habit is fixable. But you can't fix one you can't see, and you won't see it from
memory.

**2. It's the evidence at Day 90.** When you re-score
[`baseline.md`](../../05-self-assessment/baseline.md), this file is the difference between
"I think I improved" and knowing exactly where and why. It's also the answer to "what turned
out to be harder than you expected," which is one of the two questions waiting for you there.

---

## How to keep it

A minute per project, right after the write-up. Suggested shape — but any shape you'll
actually maintain beats the one you won't:

```markdown
## T1-1 · Account tour — 14 Mar

**I predicted:** mostly search, one PMax, brand a small share of spend.
**Actually:** three PMax campaigns, and brand was a much bigger share than I guessed.
**Why I was off:** I assumed PMax was a small experiment. It's most of how this
account runs, and I'd have known that from the primer if I'd connected it.
**The habit to watch:** I default to search being the main thing because it's what
I learned first.
```

The last line is the one that earns its place. **The gap is data; the habit behind it is the
lesson.**

---

## Three things worth writing down even though they'll feel small

- **When you were right for the wrong reason.** Easy to miss and just as useful — a correct
  answer from a broken model will fail you later, on something bigger.
- **When you didn't have a prediction at all.** "No idea" is a legitimate starting point, and
  the day you *do* have an instinct about that same thing is a real, datable milestone.
- **When someone corrected you and you didn't follow why.** That one's a question, not a
  gap — put it in `../questions/` too, and come back to it.

---

## One thing to be clear about

Being wrong a lot in your first months is the plan working, not the plan failing. Every
project built on predict-then-compare **expects** the gap — if there's no gap, the project
was too easy and we scoped it badly.

A long file here at Day 90 is a good outcome. A short one usually means the predictions were
being written after the fact.
