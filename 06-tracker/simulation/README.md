# Campaign Simulator

A sandbox where you build a Google Ads search campaign from scratch, launch it, and optimize
it over 12 simulated weeks — for a fictional company, with fake traffic, so nothing you do
here touches a real account or costs a real dollar.

It exists because every project on the ladder starts from an account that already exists. You
audit someone else's keywords, rewrite someone else's ad copy, fix someone else's conversion
values. That's most of the job — but it's not all of it. Building a campaign from a blank page
uses a different muscle, and this is where you practice it before it's a real account with a
real budget behind it.

---

## How to open it

Two files, both plain HTML — open them in a browser, no install:

- **`sim.html`** — the simulator itself. Start here.
- **`landing-page.html`** — the fake landing page for the company you're advertising.
  The simulator links to it; keep it open in a second tab while you write your ad copy, the
  same way you'd have a real landing page open.

If you've cloned the repo, double-click `sim.html`. If Alex has hosted it somewhere, he'll
send you the link.

> ⚠️ **Nothing here is real.** Apex Fire Solutions is invented. The traffic is generated by a
> script. The numbers are modeled, not measured. Don't quote anything from this simulator as
> if it were a Brady number — the whole point is that it's a safe place to be wrong.

## The scenario

You're running paid search for **Apex Fire Solutions** — a commercial fire protection company
that sells suppression system installs, compliance audits, inspections, and maintenance
contracts. B2B only: the buyers are facility managers and safety directors, not homeowners.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Budget** | $2,000 per simulated week |
| **Target CPA** | $150 per qualified lead |
| **Conversion** | A lead form — the "Request an Assessment" form on the landing page |
| **Run length** | 12 weeks, one week at a time |

> **Six industries, one set of mechanics.** The briefing screen has a picker — fire protection
> is the default, but you can also run commercial HVAC, industrial labeling, lab supply,
> janitorial supply, or managed IT. Each is a different world of search terms, gray-zone
> queries and deal sizes, so the *judgment* you practice transfers instead of you memorizing
> one account. Run it more than once across different verticals. Only fire protection has a
> rendered landing page; the others give you a landing-page brief to design against.

---

## What you actually do

1. **Build it.** Add keywords to three ad groups, each with a match type. Write a Responsive
   Search Ad for each group. Set your bids.
2. **Launch it.** Advance one week. The simulator generates a week of search traffic based on
   your keywords and match types.
3. **Read the search terms.** See the actual queries that triggered your ads — some are buyers,
   some are job seekers, some are people shopping for a backyard fire pit. Add the junk as
   negative keywords.
4. **Optimize.** Pause keywords that aren't working, add negatives, adjust as you learn.
5. **Repeat** for 12 weeks, then read your scorecard.

---

## What it models — and the lessons hiding in each

This isn't a toy. The things that make B2B paid search hard are built into the engine on
purpose:

- **Match types behave like the real thing.** Exact reaches only that query. Phrase reaches
  queries containing it. Broad reaches anything related — more volume, more waste. Go
  all-broad and watch what shows up in your search terms.
- **The B2B conversion delay is real here.** Leads don't convert the week they click. Some
  land the same week; most take two to eight weeks — because that's how B2B buying works
  (see [`how-brady-measures.md`](../../02-learning/how-brady-measures.md), the long-buying-cycle
  section). **Your CPA in weeks 1–3 will look awful.** That's the lag, not the campaign. If you
  panic and gut your keywords in week 2, you'll be cutting the ones about to convert. Watch the
  "pending conversions" counter — that's revenue in the pipeline.
- **Traffic comes in waves.** Week 7 spikes (think trade-show season). Week 10 dips. A single
  bad week is usually the wave, not your work. Look at the trend, not the point.
- **Budget can run out.** If your keywords are too broad and your bids too high, you'll burn
  the weekly budget on junk before the good queries ever get a chance.
- **The gray zone is in there.** Queries like "fire safety training course" and "OSHA
  regulations" — commercially B2B, but not someone buying a suppression system. Whether to
  block them is a judgment call, which is the point (see
  [`who-else-is-searching.md`](../../02-learning/who-else-is-searching.md)).

## The scorecard

At week 12 you get graded on five things, 100 points total:

| Dimension | Points | What it rewards |
|-----------|:------:|-----------------|
| CPA vs target | 25 | Coming in at or under $150 |
| Waste blocked | 25 | Catching the job-seeker, residential and irrelevant queries with negatives |
| High-intent coverage | 20 | Having keywords that actually reach the buyers |
| Ad copy | 15 | Real RSAs — enough headlines and descriptions, in all three ad groups |
| Efficiency (pipeline ROAS) | 15 | Capturing the high-value queries, not just cheap clicks |

> **There's no "spend the whole budget" score, on purpose.** B2B search is usually
> traffic-constrained, not budget-constrained — there just aren't that many people searching
> "commercial fire suppression install" this week. A tight campaign that can't spend the full
> budget isn't failing; it's being honest about how much real demand exists. Chasing budget
> spend by going broad is how you end up paying for fire-pit shoppers.

The grade isn't the point — the **reflection questions** under it are. Run it more than once.
Try a tight exact/phrase build one time and an all-broad build the next, and watch the search
terms report tell two completely different stories.

## For maintainers

`sim.html` and `landing-page.html` are self-contained — all CSS and JS inline, no server, no
dependencies. Open `sim.html` and it runs.

**The files:**

| File | What it is |
|------|-----------|
| `sim.html` | The simulator. Engine, UI, and the embedded scenario data all inline. |
| `landing-page.html` | The rendered landing page for the fire-protection vertical only. |
| `scenario-corpus.json` | **The source of truth** for the six verticals — every query, label, negative-keyword answer key and landing brief. |
| `validate-corpus.mjs` | Checks the corpus against the rules (schema, waste zeroed, gray coverage, and the load-bearing one: a negative must never be a substring of a buyer query). |
| `build-sim.mjs` | Embeds `scenario-corpus.json` into `sim.html` between the `CORPUS_START/END` markers. |

**To change the scenarios** — edit `scenario-corpus.json`, then:

```
node 06-tracker/simulation/validate-corpus.mjs 06-tracker/simulation/scenario-corpus.json
node 06-tracker/simulation/build-sim.mjs
```

Validate first (it exits non-zero on any hard failure), then build, then commit both the JSON
and the rebuilt `sim.html`. The browser can't `fetch()` the JSON from a local `file://`, which
is why the corpus is embedded rather than loaded at runtime — `build-sim.mjs` keeps the JSON as
the single source and `sim.html` as the shipped artifact.

- `CONFIG` (near the top of the `<script>` in `sim.html`) holds the budget, target CPA, traffic
  waves, conversion-delay distribution and scoring thresholds. `QUERIES` there is the
  fire-protection fallback used only if the corpus hasn't been embedded.
- The engine (`simulateWeek`, `getTotalStats`, `calculateScore`) is pure and DOM-free, so it
  can be exercised headlessly by extracting the `<script>` and stubbing `document` — that's how
  the corpus and scenario switching were smoke-tested.
- This folder is deliberately outside the content build (`06-tracker` is excluded), so nothing
  here is swept into the Apps Script web app. It's a standalone browser tool, like the rest of
  the tracker.
