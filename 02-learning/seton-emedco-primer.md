# Seton / EMEDCO Primer — the account group you're contributing to

Seton and EMEDCO are the second account group on your ramp — you're a **contributor** here
(audit, propose, help), not an owner, so this primer stays at orientation level: what the
business is, how the two accounts differ, and the things that will trip up a newcomer.
Read [`ppc-fundamentals.md`](./ppc-fundamentals.md) first if you haven't.

---

## What Seton and EMEDCO are

Seton and EMEDCO sit alongside Brady and PDC as one of the team's account groups, run
under a separate manager account (see the MCC-naming trap in
[`cheat-sheet.md`](./cheat-sheet.md) — the MCC that holds these two is confusingly named
"Brady US - MCC (Seton/Emed)" and does **not** contain the actual Brady US account).

Both sell industrial safety and identification products — think pipe & valve markers,
safety and facility signs, traffic control products, labels, tags, and lockout/tagout
gear — to a B2B buyer. **Seton** (seton.com) is the larger of the two; **EMEDCO**
(emedco.com) is smaller and runs a leaner, more Shopping-heavy program. Both are separate
Google Ads accounts, run together under the same manager account alongside Seton Canada.

## How the two accounts differ

| | Seton US | EMEDCO |
|---|---|---|
| Size | The larger account | Smaller |
| Channel mix | A real two-engine account — Search and Shopping run at comparable scale | Shopping-first, with a small brand/search layer on top |
| Timezone | Eastern | Central |
| What that means for you | More search structure to learn (match-type splits, DSA coverage) | Simpler shape, but don't mistake "simpler" for "smaller effort" — Shopping tuning still matters a lot here |

**Timezone mismatch matters:** Seton reports on Eastern time, EMEDCO on Central. If you're
comparing "yesterday's" numbers across the two, or scheduling anything, account for the
gap — it's a real source of confusing day-over-day comparisons for a newcomer.

## ⚠️ Before you trust a number on this account group

The same rule from [`how-brady-measures.md`](./how-brady-measures.md) applies here, with
an extra wrinkle: **platform ROAS on Seton/EMEDCO is not a clean revenue signal.** These
two accounts bid on a blended mix that includes actual purchases *and* placeholder values
for leads (quote requests, catalog requests, phone calls) *and* an offline-value import.
That mix inflates and complicates the platform ROAS number well beyond the usual
first-touch-vs-platform gap. **Use platform ROAS to compare campaigns against each other,
never to report as revenue.** For real revenue, go through the same first-touch,
BigQuery/Adobe path as everywhere else.

Seton and EMEDCO also each carry **a large amount of paused and legacy campaign
structure** underneath the live accounts — old builds from earlier campaign-management
generations that are switched off but still present. **Never bulk-enable anything paused
without checking with Alex or the account lead first** — some of it is inert history, but
some of it exists for reasons that aren't obvious just from the name.

## What "good" looks like on this account group

- Seton runs closer to breakeven on platform ROAS than Brady's flagship accounts do —
  that's expected, not a red flag. Efficiency profiles differ a lot by account group; see
  [`how-brady-measures.md`](./how-brady-measures.md) for the guardrail concept and don't
  compare Seton/EMEDCO's numbers raw against Brady's.
- EMEDCO tends to run more efficiently than Seton on platform ROAS, partly because it
  leans harder on Shopping and carries a smaller, thinner search layer.
- Brand campaigns on both accounts are intentionally throttled in places — a low ROAS on
  a brand campaign here can be a deliberate spend cap, not underperformance. Ask before
  assuming something's broken.

## Where to go deeper

This primer is deliberately light — Seton/EMEDCO is a big, busy account group, and the
full operational picture (campaign naming conventions, budget-tier structure, negative-list
architecture, audience setup) is something you'll pick up hands-on as you start
contributing, not something to memorize up front. When you're ready to go deeper:

- Ask **Alex** or your account lead for a walkthrough of the live campaign list — the
  campaigns that actually spend meaningfully are a small fraction of what's in the account.
- **Ben** (SEO) has worked on both PDC and Seton/EMEDCO and is worth an early conversation
  about the business side (see
  [`../01-start-here/how-the-ramp-works.md`](../01-start-here/how-the-ramp-works.md)).
- Bring specific questions to your 1:1s rather than trying to absorb the whole account
  structure solo — this group has a lot of history, and the fastest way through it is
  asking someone who was there.
