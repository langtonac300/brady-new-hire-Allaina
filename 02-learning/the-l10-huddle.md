# The L10 huddle

The team's weekly meeting, in depth — because it's the one meeting where your work becomes
visible to everyone, and because it runs on rules that aren't obvious from the outside.

> **Tuesday, 10:00.** Alex facilitates. Courtney, Scott, CJ and you. It runs inside the
> **L10 Huddle app**, which lives in the MTD Spend workbook — Alex will show you where.

---

## What "L10" means

It's short for **Level 10**, from a management system called EOS. The idea is blunt: most
recurring team meetings are bad, so you fix them by running the *same agenda in the same
order every single week* and then asking everyone to rate the meeting 1–10 on the way out.
The name is the target. A meeting that consistently earns an 8 or better is doing its job.

**Why this matters to you specifically:** in a company this size it is genuinely possible to
work hard for a month and have nobody know what you did. The L10 is the structural fix for
that. Your to-dos, your numbers and eventually your rocks are all read out loud, weekly, in
front of the whole team.

That sounds intimidating for about three weeks and then becomes the thing you'd miss most.

---

## The seven segments

The app runs a countdown on each one. The clock is the point — it's what stops the meeting
becoming an hour of the loudest topic.

| # | Segment | Min | What actually happens |
|---|---------|----:|----------------------|
| 1 | **Segue** | 5 | One personal good-news item and one work one, each. Kudos. **No work talk yet** — that's deliberate, not filler |
| 2 | **Scorecard** | 5 | The weekly numbers get read. Each is **on track or off track**. Nothing else |
| 3 | **Rock review** | 5 | Each rock owner says on track or off track. Same rule |
| 4 | **Headlines** | 5 | One-liners — customer news, people news, FYIs. Old news gets killed |
| 5 | **To-dos** | 5 | Last week's list, read out: done or not done |
| 6 | **IDS** | 60 | The actual meeting. Identify, Discuss, Solve |
| 7 | **Conclude** | 5 | New to-dos recapped, recap posted to team chat, everyone rates the meeting |

---

## Segment 2 · The scorecard, and the rule people get wrong

A handful of weekly numbers, each with an owner. Spend pacing per account group, efficiency
against its guardrail, experiments running, negatives added and the spend that saved.

**The rule: you do not discuss the scorecard during the scorecard.**

If a number is off track, you say "off track" and it **drops down** to the issues list for
IDS. You don't explain it. You don't defend it. You don't solve it. That's the whole
conversation, and it's the only reason five minutes stays five minutes.

New people find this deeply unnatural — the instinct when your number is red is to
immediately say why. Resist it. You'll get a proper hour on it in IDS if it's important
enough to win a vote, and if it isn't important enough to win a vote, it didn't need the
explanation.

> ⚠️ **Every number carries its caveat, read out with it.** Especially anything
> conversion-flavored while tracking fixes are pending. A number without its caveat is worse
> than no number, because people act on it. See
> [`how-brady-measures.md`](./how-brady-measures.md).

## Segment 3 · Rocks

A **rock** is a quarterly commitment — something meaningful you'll finish in about 90 days.
One to three per person, due-dated, each tagged to one of the team's four strategic shifts.
They're set in a working session at the fiscal-quarter turn, not invented in the huddle.

Rocks carry dated milestones plotted across their life, so "on track" is checkable rather
than a feeling. Ticking the last milestone closes the rock.

**You will not have a rock for a while**, and that's correct — a rock is a 90-day promise and
you're still learning what 90 days of your own output looks like. Expect your first one
somewhere after the ramp. Until then you'll hear other people's and start to learn what a
well-sized one looks like.

## Segment 4 · Headlines

One-liners only. Something a customer said, someone's news, an FYI. If it needs a decision,
it isn't a headline — it's an issue.

Anything worth passing up to Stuart's wider digital team gets flagged to **cascade**, and
Alex carries it there. Old headlines get killed off the list deliberately, so the list stays
a signal.

## Segment 5 · To-dos

Last week's list, read out: **done or not done.**

| The rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| **One owner, seven days** | "We'll all look at it" means nobody will. Every to-do has exactly one name |
| **Team target is ≥90% done** | It is a **team** number, always. There is no per-person completion percentage anywhere in the system, on purpose |
| **"Working on it" is not done** | Neither is "blocked." Both are legitimate — just say which one, and who you're waiting on |
| **Three carry-overs and it gets re-filed** | The app flags it and offers two doors: make it a rock, or take it to IDS. Work that survives three weeks was never a seven-day commitment — that's a scoping problem, not a character problem |

**The to-do list is a daily surface, not a Tuesday ritual.** The team spends most of its time
in it. It has search, **Mine / Overdue / Next 7 days** filters, three states (open, working,
blocked), snooze buttons, sub-steps for breaking a big one down, and an append-only notes
trail so the story of a task lives on the task instead of scattered through chat.

**Marking something blocked posts to the team space**, so a hand-off doesn't sit until
Tuesday. Use it. It is not an admission of anything.

## Segment 6 · IDS — the hour that matters

Sixty of the ninety minutes. **Identify, Discuss, Solve.**

**How it works:** issues have accumulated all week — dropped down from the scorecard, raised
by anyone, or added from a desk on a Thursday. Then:

1. **Voting.** The app picks someone at random to start, then goes around the room in turn.
   **Three votes each**, stacked on one issue or spread across three. The list re-orders
   itself by votes.
2. **Take the top one.** **Identify** the actual root cause — usually not the thing that was
   written down. **Discuss** it once, properly. **Solve** it.
3. **The solution becomes a to-do** with one owner and a date.
4. Repeat down the list until the hour goes.

**"Solve forever, not for this week."** If the fix wouldn't stop it happening again, it isn't
a solve — it's a patch, and the issue will be back next month wearing a different hat.

Three things that keep IDS honest:

- **An issue is not a status update.** If it doesn't need a decision or a solution, it's a
  headline.
- **Two-person topics get parked to a 1:1.** A thing that only concerns you and Alex should
  not spend four people's hour. The app parks it to that 1:1 for you.
- **If the answer is "we should test that,"** it goes to the Experiment Hub's ideas backlog,
  which is where tests actually live.

**You get three votes from your first meeting.** Not after you've earned them. If you don't
yet know what matters most, vote for the thing you understood least — that's genuinely useful
information for the room.

## Segment 7 · Conclude

New to-dos read back, the recap goes to the team chat, anything cascading is set, and
everyone rates the meeting 1–10.

**Rate it honestly.** Below an 8, you say where it lost you. A new person saying "6, I didn't
follow the second half" is not a complaint — it's the single most useful data point the
facilitator gets, because everyone else has stopped noticing.

## The house rules, in one place

1. The scorecard never gets discussed in the scorecard segment. "Drop it down" is the whole conversation.
2. Every number carries its caveat.
3. One owner per to-do, seven days.
4. Issues are not status updates.
5. Two-person topics get parked to a 1:1.
6. Solved means written down — resolution logged, actions owned and dated.
7. Test-shaped issues go to the Experiment Hub.
8. **Off track is information, not an accusation.**

That last one is the culture of the meeting and it's worth reading twice. **Saying "off
track" early is the move that earns trust here.** Surprises at the deadline are what cost it.
Nobody in this room is impressed by a number that was green for six weeks and then wasn't.

## What's expected of you, by when

| Phase | What you do in the huddle |
|-------|--------------------------|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Sit in. Listen. Bring a segue item — that one you can do from day one, and it's how the room learns who you are. Ask afterward about anything you didn't follow |
| **Weeks 4–8** | You'll start carrying to-dos of your own. Read your own status honestly. Start voting in IDS |
| **Weeks 8–13** | You own a line on the scorecard and read your own number. You raise issues rather than only answering them |
| **After the ramp** | A rock of your own. You bring solutions to IDS rather than only problems |

---

## The two mistakes new people make

**1. Explaining a red number during the scorecard.** Covered above. Everyone does it once.

**2. Marking a to-do done when it's mostly done.** The ≥90% target creates a small, real pull
toward optimism. Don't. A team number that's honest is useful and a team number that's
flattering is worse than nothing — and the whole reason the target is a *team* number, with
no per-person breakdown anywhere, is so that nobody has an incentive to fudge their own row.
